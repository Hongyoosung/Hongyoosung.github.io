### The Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman Equation: Foundation for Continuous Reinforcement Learning
**Link:** https://dani2442.github.io/posts/continuous-rl/

At the heart of optimal control theory lies the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation, which serves as the continuous-time counterpart to the discrete-time Bellman equation. This partial differential equation (PDE) describes the optimal value function in continuous optimal control problems, extending the dynamic programming principle to infinitesimal time steps. Remarkably, Bellman himself discovered its structural identity with the 19th-century Hamilton-Jacobi equation from classical mechanics.

For engineers, the HJB equation provides a rigorous mathematical framework to analyze and design optimal control strategies for systems operating in continuous state and action spaces, moving beyond discrete approximations. It’s an invaluable analytical tool for understanding system optimality, robustness, and inherent trade-offs in dynamic environments, with its connection to classical physics offering deeper theoretical insights into system dynamics. Practically, HJB theory is leveraged to design highly optimized continuous control policies for complex real-world systems, such as precise trajectory planning in robotics, autonomous vehicle navigation, and continuous-flow process optimization. Furthermore, understanding its principles can inform the development and theoretical analysis of advanced machine learning techniques, particularly in interpreting and potentially improving generative diffusion models through the lens of stochastic optimal control.

### See, Symbolize, Act: Grounding VLMs with Spatial Representations for Better Gameplay
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11601

Enhancing the ability of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to perform precise actions is critical for interactive AI systems. Recent research highlights the benefit of augmenting visual frames with symbolic scene representations to improve VLM performance. Evaluations across diverse interactive environments, using various input pipelines (frame-only, frame + self-extracted symbols, frame + ground-truth symbols), consistently demonstrate that accurate symbolic information significantly boosts performance. However, relying on VLMs to self-extract symbols often introduces a performance bottleneck due to limitations in perception quality.

For engineers, this research underscores the necessity of high-quality, reliable symbolic grounding for robust VLM-based action execution, transcending mere visual scene description. It identifies the VLM's inherent capability to accurately extract symbolic information from visual inputs as a critical bottleneck, suggesting a need for more robust perception front-ends or explicit symbolic reasoning architectures. When building interactive VLM systems, such as for robotic control or game AI, engineers should prioritize robust symbol extraction, potentially by integrating dedicated computer vision modules for object detection and state estimation. This allows for the provision of accurate symbolic context, leading to a hybrid architecture where reliable symbolic data augments VLM visual processing for critical action grounding.

### Extreme Value Monte Carlo Tree Search for Classical Planning
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18248

Classical planning often faces challenges with unbounded cost-to-go estimates and under-specified reward support when using existing Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) algorithms in Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS). A novel bandit algorithm, UCB1-Uniform, addresses these issues by leveraging Peaks-Over-Threshold Extreme Value Theory. This approach provides theoretical justification for Bellman backup in MCTS, significantly enhancing its performance in complex planning tasks.

This work offers a substantial improvement for engineers developing MCTS-based planning systems, especially in domains characterized by potentially unbounded cost estimates. By providing a theoretically sound method to handle such costs, it enhances the reliability and efficiency of AI planning agents, leading to more robust and optimal decision-making in complex operational environments. Engineers can apply the UCB1-Uniform algorithm in MCTS implementations for various real-world planning problems, including optimizing resource allocation, robotic mission planning, or logistics scheduling where costs are unpredictable and potentially high. Integrating this method can lead to more effective and resilient automated planning solutions in practical engineering applications.

### R-PGA: Robust Physical Adversarial Camouflage Generation via Relightable 3D Gaussian Splatting
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26067

The fragility of physical adversarial camouflage in complex real-world scenarios poses a significant threat to AI systems, particularly in critical applications like autonomous driving. R-PGA addresses this by enhancing both simulation fidelity and optimization robustness. It utilizes Relightable 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to achieve photo-realistic scene reconstruction with physically disentangled material and lighting attributes, combined with a hybrid rendering pipeline for accurate relighted foregrounds and backgrounds. To ensure attack robustness, the Hard Physical Configuration Mining (HPCM) module actively identifies worst-case physical configurations and flattens the corresponding loss peaks, guaranteeing consistent adversarial effectiveness across diverse conditions.

This work significantly advances the realism and robustness of adversarial attack generation, which is crucial for evaluating and securing AI systems in critical applications. By employing Relightable 3DGS, R-PGA provides a superior, physically accurate simulation environment that minimizes the domain gap between synthetic and real-world conditions, yielding more generalizable adversarial textures. The HPCM optimization strategy addresses a key limitation in current adversarial research by ensuring attacks remain effective despite real-world geometric and radiometric variations. Engineers can leverage R-PGA to conduct advanced security assessments and stress tests on autonomous vehicle perception systems, generating highly robust physical adversarial camouflages for various objects under a wide range of environmental conditions. This framework enables the creation of realistic test scenarios to identify vulnerabilities in object detection and classification models under dynamic lighting, viewing angles, and atmospheric effects, serving as a powerful tool for developing more resilient AI architectures and robust adversarial defense mechanisms.
