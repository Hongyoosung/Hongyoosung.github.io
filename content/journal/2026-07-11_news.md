### ARDY: Autoregressive Diffusion with Hybrid Representation for Interactive Human Motion Generation
First, let's explore the dynamic world of interactive human motion. **ARDY** introduces a streaming generation framework for real-time, high-fidelity 3D human motion, controllable via online text prompts and kinematic constraints. It employs a clever hybrid representation, merging explicit root features with a latent body embedding, striking a balance between precise trajectory control and efficient generative learning. Powered by a two-stage autoregressive transformer denoiser, ARDY tackles the long-standing challenge of achieving both real-time inference and precise, high-fidelity control. This breakthrough is set to revolutionize interactive animation systems in video games, virtual reality, and the metaverse, allowing for dynamic character control via text prompts and kinematic constraints. It also holds significant promise for humanoid robotics, enabling more naturalistic human-robot interaction and sophisticated motion planning, as well as for advanced simulation environments requiring realistic human agent behavior.

**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08741

### HoloTetSphere: Unified TetSphere Mesh Reconstruction for Physical Simulations
Moving from dynamic motion to static, yet deformable, structures, **HoloTetSphere** addresses a critical bottleneck in simulation workflows. This innovative framework delivers holistic tetrahedral mesh reconstruction by directly optimizing volumetric primitives, circumventing the conventional, often error-prone, two-stage process of surface extraction and subsequent tetrahedralization. By coupling Gaussian spheres to tetrahedral elements and utilizing edge connections to estimate a continuous opacity field for differentiable element pruning, HoloTetSphere enables end-to-end topological and geometric optimization. The result is unified, topologically coherent tetrahedral meshes with superior geometric accuracy, directly usable in advanced physical simulations. For engineers, this means a significant reduction in time and resources for workflows involving Finite Element Analysis (FEA) for structural integrity, fluid dynamics, or thermal simulations, virtual prototyping, and realistic animation, streamlining the creation of robust simulation models from raw data.

**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08398

### LightCrafter: PBR-Conditioned Video Diffusion Refinement for Controllable and Consistent Relighting
Next, we delve into the visual fidelity of digital content with **LightCrafter**. This hybrid video relighting pipeline masterfully combines the strengths of Physically-Based Rendering (PBR) and data-driven generative diffusion models. LightCrafter first generates a PBR "proxy video" under the desired target illumination, which inherently incorporates illumination targets and provides robust temporal consistency. This proxy is then meticulously refined by a post-trained video diffusion model (such as CogVideoX), adding complex global illumination effects and enhancing photorealism. This ingenious approach sidesteps common issues like noisy reconstructions, global illumination challenges, and temporal instability found in purely generative methods, offering unprecedented control and consistency in video relighting. Senior engineers in media, entertainment, virtual production, and extended reality (XR) can leverage LightCrafter for advanced post-production, seamlessly relighting captured content to match arbitrary virtual environments and significantly boosting immersion.

**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08016

### LLM-powered reasoning in agent-based modeling
Shifting gears to complex system analysis, we find a groundbreaking integration in **LLM-powered reasoning in agent-based modeling**. This research introduces the Hybrid Agent-based and Language-driven Epidemic (HALE) framework, which marries Large Language Models (LLMs) with traditional Agent-Based Models (ABMs). The core innovation lies in leveraging LLMs to dynamically predict human decision-making within simulations, enabling agents to adapt their behavior in real-time. This dynamic approach dramatically overcomes the rigidity of conventional ABMs that rely on static, pre-defined rules, leading to far more robust and accurate predictive models for systems where human interaction is a key variable. Engineers working on high-fidelity simulations for urban planning, public health response modeling (e.g., pandemic spread), or smart city infrastructure design can apply this to test the impact of different interventions or policies, designing more resilient and adaptive systems that account for unpredictable human factors.

**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06757

### Grounding Spatial Relations in a Compact World Model: Instruction Leakage and a Goal-Free Dynamics Fix
Finally, addressing a crucial challenge in building truly intelligent agents, particularly in robotics, is the work on **Grounding Spatial Relations in a Compact World Model: Instruction Leakage and a Goal-Free Dynamics Fix**. This paper meticulously identifies "instruction leakage" – a phenomenon where world models using language goals for spatial relation grounding appear accurate by merely transcribing the instruction, rather than genuinely perceiving the environment, often collapsing to chance when the goal is withheld. Crucially, they propose a technical fix: decoupling the language goal from the world model's internal dynamics and instead treating it as a cost for the planner, while rigorously supervising the perceptual "read path." This research is vital for engineers developing robust AI systems, as it exposes a common pitfall leading to brittle and unpredictable behavior in real-world applications. By implementing the diagnostic protocol and architectural fix, engineers can build world models that are more interpretable, generalize better, and genuinely interact with the physical world based on language, especially in applications like robotic manipulation and virtual agent control.

**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06925
