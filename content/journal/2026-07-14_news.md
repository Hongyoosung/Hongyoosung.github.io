### The art and engineering of Sega CD Silpheed
**Link:** https://fabiensanglard.net/silpheed/index.html

Silpheed stands as a testament to extreme optimization, achieving its visually striking animations on the Sega CD through a meticulously engineered Full Motion Video (FMV) format. Despite severe hardware limitations—a 12.5MHz m68k CPU, only 16 colors, and a single-speed 150 KiB/s CD-ROM—the game delivered near-fullscreen cutscenes at 15fps, leaving a mere 8 KiB per video frame after accommodating music. This engineering feat underscores the critical role of custom data formats and compression algorithms when off-the-shelf solutions fall short. The principles demonstrated by Silpheed’s FMV optimization remain highly relevant for resource-constrained projects today, such as embedded systems or specialized data streaming, offering a valuable methodology for designing custom codecs, optimizing data pipelines, and prioritizing information delivery under severe bandwidth or processing limitations.

### WAM-TTT: Steering World-Action Models by Watching Human Play at Test Time
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06988

WAM-TTT introduces an innovative test-time training framework that allows pre-trained World-Action Models (WAMs) to be steered using raw human videos, eliminating the need for additional robot demonstrations or human annotations. Instead of direct imitation, it updates a lightweight adaptive memory within a *frozen* WAM through self-supervised video prediction. A preceding meta-training stage, leveraging paired human-robot data and a key-value memory reconstruction objective, ensures this adaptive memory effectively bridges human demonstrations with robot behaviors for precise control. This framework offers a highly efficient and reusable method for adapting robot foundation models, significantly reducing engineering costs and preserving generalization ability. Practically, WAM-TTT enables robotic systems to rapidly adapt to novel task variations or user preferences by simply observing human video, making it invaluable for customized assembly, personalized service robotics, or dynamic environments requiring swift skill adaptation.

### Linux on the Sega 32X. Who needs hardware synchronization primitives anyway?
**Link:** https://cakehonolulu.github.io/linux-on-32x/

Porting Linux to retro hardware like the Sega 32X might seem niche, but it's a profound exercise in enhancing fundamental board bringup skills for firmware and embedded systems engineering. This endeavor demands extensive investigation, experimentation with emulators, and deep dives into often poorly documented technical specifications, including reverse-engineering existing boot images. The methodology emphasizes adapting core OS components like the kernel and bootloader to specific hardware quirks, mirroring the challenges faced when porting Android 5.0 to an unsupported MediaTek phone. This work highlights the critical importance of hands-on board bringup experience, fostering proficiency in navigating incomplete documentation, system-level debugging, and iterative hardware-software integration. Engineers can directly apply these problem-solving skills to developing custom embedded devices, porting operating systems to new chipsets, or integrating complex peripheral drivers in IoT, automotive, and industrial control domains.

### PAC-ACT: Post-training Actor-Critic for Action Chunking Transformers
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09590

PAC-ACT is a post-training reinforcement learning framework designed to enhance the robustness of pretrained Action Chunking Transformer (ACT) policies, particularly for contact-rich tasks where distribution shift often degrades performance. It reformulates policy optimization at the chunk level through an ACT-transferred actor-critic architecture, incorporating a hybrid behavior-prior constraint to maintain the pretrained action distribution during online fine-tuning. This framework significantly boosts the reliability and safety of real-time industrial robot control, enabling robust performance under pose perturbations and strict contact-force constraints without increasing latency or GPU-memory footprint. Experiments have shown dramatic improvements, including a 46x decrease in dangerous peak contact forces. PAC-ACT is ideal for fine-tuning industrial robots performing high-precision contact tasks like assembly or insertion, ensuring robust and safe operations even with common pose perturbations and critical contact-force limits.

### GATS: Graph-Augmented Tree Search with Layered World Models for Efficient Agent Planning
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08894

GATS (Graph-Augmented Tree Search) revolutionizes planning for LLM agents by replacing expensive, stochastic LLM inference *during planning* with a systematic, UCB1-based tree search. It employs a three-layer world model that combines exact symbolic matching, statistics learned from execution logs, and a fallback LLM for unknown actions. This architecture enables highly efficient, deterministic planning, dramatically reducing the number of LLM calls required per task. This innovation significantly cuts computational costs and eliminates the inherent stochasticity of LLM-heavy planning agents, leading to predictable and reliable behavior. By offloading much of the planning to a structured, learned world model, GATS achieves superior success rates and efficiency compared to prior LLM-centric approaches, especially in complex, long-horizon tasks. Engineers can deploy GATS to create robust autonomous agents for critical multi-step tasks such as automated software development or infrastructure management, where high reliability and explainable behavior are paramount.
