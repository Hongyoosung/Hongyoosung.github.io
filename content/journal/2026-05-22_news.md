In the rapidly evolving landscape of artificial intelligence, researchers are pushing the boundaries of what's possible in character animation, interactive environment creation, and robotic control. From enabling ultra-realistic virtual agents to generating physics-grounded 3D scenes from single images, and teaching humanoids complex skills from video, these innovations are poised to transform industries.

### NaP-Control: Navigating Diffusion Prior for Versatile and Fast Character Control
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20209

NaP-Control introduces a groundbreaking approach to precise, versatile whole-body character control. It leverages reinforcement learning to directly manipulate the latent noise of a task-agnostic diffusion policy prior, steering it toward task-specific behaviors. This innovation bypasses the need for slow, iterative gradient-based guidance during denoising by directly predicting task-optimized diffusion noise. During training, the method interactively optimizes task rewards and corrects motions by engaging with the environment.

From an engineering perspective, NaP-Control delivers substantial improvements in efficiency and robustness for physics-based character animation. Its direct prediction of optimized diffusion noise leads to faster inference and higher success rates, essential for real-time applications and complex scenarios where previous diffusion-based policies often struggled with speed and reliability. Practically, this technology is invaluable for game development, enabling highly realistic and responsive AI characters. It also extends to robotics for complex manipulation and locomotion, and to VR/AR for naturalistic avatar control, allowing for characters that perform diverse, challenging tasks with high motion fidelity and adaptability without compromising performance.

### TelePhysics: Physics-Grounded Multi-Object Scene Generation from a Single Image with Real-Time Interaction
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20290

TelePhysics offers a training-free framework that transforms a single 2D image into a physically consistent and controllable video through holistic scene-level 3D reconstruction. This is achieved by representing the entire scene geometry within a unified spatial coordinate system, effectively resolving common issues like object interpenetration and spatial misalignment found in previous methods. A key innovation is decoupling simulation from rendering, which allows for real-time physical interaction previews while maintaining photorealistic visual fidelity.

This framework marks a significant leap in generative AI by tackling critical limitations in physical consistency and controllability for dynamic 3D scenes. Its unified scene representation enables accurate multi-object physics interactions and more complex control, vital for reliable simulations and interactive systems. The real-time, low-latency interaction capability, born from the simulation-rendering decoupling, provides substantial improvements for rapid iteration and development. Engineers can use TelePhysics to quickly generate interactive, physics-grounded 3D scenes from a single image for diverse applications, including realistic simulation environments for robotics and autonomous systems, advanced virtual prototyping, and dynamic, controllable assets for gaming and extended reality (XR) experiences.

### SUGAR: A Scalable Human-Video-Driven Generalizable Humanoid Loco-Manipulation Learning Framework
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20373

SUGAR introduces a three-stage framework designed to convert diverse human videos into deployable humanoid loco-manipulation skills. Initially, it extracts kinematic interaction priors from unstructured videos. These imperfect priors are then refined into physically feasible, high-fidelity skills using a physics-based refiner. Finally, these refined skills are distilled into a hierarchical autonomous policy, ready for real-world execution.

This framework tackles the formidable challenge of generalizable humanoid control by eliminating the laborious process of task-specific reward engineering and the dependence on perfect reference motions. It robustly translates imperfect human video data into real-world transferable skills, consistently outperforming traditional baselines and demonstrating clear performance scaling with increased data volume. Senior Engineers can leverage SUGAR to rapidly develop and deploy complex humanoid robot behaviors directly from human video demonstrations, drastically cutting down development time and resource overhead. Its capability for zero-shot real-world transfer, coupled with autonomous failure recovery and long-horizon stability, makes it an ideal solution for robustly integrating humanoid robots into dynamic, unstructured environments without extensive manual tuning.

### For How Long Should We Be Punching? Learning Action Duration in Fighting Games
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20911

This paper introduces a novel reinforcement learning (RL) framework that empowers an agent to predict not only *what* action to take but also *for how long* to execute it. This allows agents to dynamically adjust their responsiveness to various game situations, moving beyond conventional fixed-interval decision-making. Through experiments in a fighting game environment, the research demonstrates that this learned timing can match the performance of well-chosen fixed frame skips and fosters repeatable action patterns.

Technically, this approach offers a significant enhancement for real-time RL systems, enabling agents to develop more nuanced, human-like timing that balances responsiveness with computational efficiency. Engineers can utilize this to create more adaptable and less "robotic" AI behavior, although the research notes it doesn't inherently guarantee robustness and can sometimes favor less responsive, exploitative strategies against scripted opponents. Beyond gaming, this methodology is applicable to any control system requiring dynamic timing, such as robotics for more fluid and situation-aware movements, or autonomous systems (e.g., vehicles, drones) to optimize the duration of control inputs, ultimately enhancing performance and efficiency in simulations and real-world industrial automation.
