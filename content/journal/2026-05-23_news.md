In the rapidly evolving landscape of artificial intelligence and computer graphics, researchers and engineers are consistently pushing the boundaries of what's possible. From creating more realistic game AI to enabling sophisticated humanoid robotics and optimizing real-time visual experiences, the focus remains on enhancing performance, accuracy, and adaptability. This post dives into five recent innovations that are shaping the future of AI, simulation, and real-time systems.

### For How Long Should We Be Punching? Learning Action Duration in Fighting Games
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20911

A significant challenge in real-time reinforcement learning (RL) is enabling agents to adapt their actions dynamically in fast-paced environments. This paper introduces an RL framework that allows agents to jointly learn not only *what* action to take but also its optimal *duration*. This mechanism moves beyond traditional fixed-interval decision-making, granting agents the ability to predict how long an action should be executed, thereby achieving more nuanced and adaptive control than conventional methods that offer either unrealistic, frame-perfect reflexes or compromise responsiveness with fixed decision intervals.

This novel approach is critical for advancing real-time RL, paving the way for more realistic and robust AI systems. By dynamically adjusting temporal behavior, agents can balance responsiveness with computational efficiency, leading to sophisticated behaviors where action timing and persistence are crucial. Practically, this methodology is invaluable for developing advanced AI in games requiring complex timing, such as sports simulations or character control. Engineers can leverage this framework to design real-time control systems where agents must dynamically balance quick reactions with sustained operations, optimizing resource allocation and improving overall system adaptability.

### Mahjax: A GPU-Accelerated Mahjong Simulator for Reinforcement Learning in JAX
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20577

Advancing "tabula rasa" (from scratch) reinforcement learning in complex, imperfect-information games often requires massive computational resources. Mahjax addresses this by presenting a fully vectorized Riichi Mahjong environment in JAX, specifically designed for GPU-accelerated large-scale RL research. It achieves massive rollout parallelization, boasting throughputs of up to 2 million steps per second on eight NVIDIA A100 GPUs, and includes a high-quality visualization tool for streamlined debugging.

This work provides a crucial tool for accelerating the development and validation of advanced AI policies in complex, multi-agent, stochastic, and imperfect-information scenarios. Its unprecedented GPU-accelerated throughput dramatically reduces the computational time for large-scale RL experiments, making it possible to effectively train agents that improve against established baselines. Engineers can utilize Mahjax as a high-performance simulation platform to rapidly prototype and test novel RL algorithms. Its applications extend beyond games to domains like resource allocation, market trading, or autonomous system control where efficient data generation is paramount, with the visualization tool further aiding in understanding complex agent behaviors.

### Accelerating 3D Gaussian Splatting using Tensor Cores
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17855

Efficient real-time neural rendering is a cornerstone for next-generation immersive experiences. TensorGS dramatically accelerates 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) by re-architecting its compute-bound rasterization stage to harness underutilized GPU Tensor Cores. The framework "tensorizes" existing irregular, per-pixel scalar operations into dense, regular matrix operations compatible with Tensor Core execution. Furthermore, it introduces cross-tile grouping to enhance Gaussian reuse, amortize overhead, and significantly improve Tensor Core utilization, enabling efficient low-precision (FP16) rendering.

This research highlights the efficient utilization of specialized hardware, demonstrating how to adapt an irregular graphics workload like 3DGS rasterization to the structured execution model of Tensor Cores. By achieving a 1.65x end-to-end rendering performance improvement while maintaining image quality, TensorGS offers a blueprint for optimizing real-time neural rendering pipelines and maximizing throughput on modern GPUs for latency-sensitive applications. Senior Engineers can integrate these principles to develop more performant real-time neural rendering and 3D scene reconstruction applications, crucial for AR/VR, robotics, high-fidelity simulations, and gaming, leading to faster content delivery and smoother user experiences.

### SONIC: Supersizing Motion Tracking for Natural Humanoid Whole-Body Control
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07820

Developing natural and robust humanoid control has long been a complex endeavor, often hampered by intricate reward engineering. SONIC introduces a foundation model for humanoid control by massively scaling network size (up to 42M parameters), data volume (700 hours of motion capture), and compute (21k GPU hours). It reframes motion tracking as a scalable task, using dense supervision from diverse mocap data to directly acquire human motion priors, thereby bypassing manual reward engineering and yielding a generalist controller for natural whole-body movements.

This research demonstrates that scaling model capacity, data, and compute is fundamental to overcoming the limitations of prior, smaller humanoid controllers, setting a new standard for policy acquisition. It simplifies the development process by replacing complex reward engineering with readily available motion data, leading to policies that show steady performance improvements with scale and generalize robustly to unseen motions. The SONIC framework can enable natural and interactive humanoid control, such as through a real-time kinematic planner for navigation. Its unified token space also supports VR teleoperation and provides a robust policy interface for vision-language-action (VLA) models, facilitating autonomous whole-body loco-manipulation requiring coordinated hand and foot placement.

### MoSA: Motion-constrained Stress Adaptation for Mitigating Real-to-Sim Gap in Continuum Dynamics via Learning Residual Anisotropy
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22597

Bridging the real-to-sim gap is a persistent challenge, especially in continuum dynamics where materials often exhibit complex, non-isotropic behaviors. MoSA is a framework designed to learn residual anisotropy and heterogeneity in materials by utilizing an isotropic physics model as a strong prior. A physics-informed cascaded network then learns to adapt and redistribute stresses via microplane constraints, incorporating motion constraints through supervision of temporal and spatial derivatives of the deformation field. This ensures that the learned residual stress operators are physically meaningful.

This approach is significant because it overcomes the limitations of traditional simulators that assume homogeneous and isotropic materials, as well as the data inefficiency of purely black-box neural networks. By integrating physical priors with learned residual effects, MoSA offers a more accurate, generalizable, and robust method for modeling complex material dynamics, which is crucial for reliable predictive simulations and reducing the disparity between simulated and real-world behavior. Senior Engineers can leverage MoSA to create highly accurate digital twins for compliant materials and soft robots, essential for enhancing sim-to-real transfer in advanced robotic manipulation tasks and control systems. This framework can improve the fidelity of simulations for predicting material deformation, optimizing designs for flexible mechanisms, and ultimately reducing the need for costly physical prototyping in fields requiring precise continuum dynamics.
