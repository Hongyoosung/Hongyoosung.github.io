### RTX 5080 and RTX 3090 Setup: 80 Tok/s on Qwen 3.6 27B Q8
**Link:** https://imil.net/blog/posts/2026/rtx-5080-+-rtx-3090-setup-80+-tok-s-on-qwen-3.6-27b-q8/

Achieving high-performance local inference for large language models often hinges on maximizing available VRAM and compute. This technical setup showcases an effective strategy to combine an RTX 5080 (16GB) and an RTX 3090 (24GB) to deliver an impressive 80+ tokens/second inference rate on the Qwen 3.6 27B Q8 model. The core innovation lies in leveraging a motherboard capable of splitting a 16x PCIe lane into 2x8x, coupled with specific BIOS configurations like enabled Above 4G Decoding and ReSize BAR Support, and the use of the `nvidia-open` driver to support this heterogeneous GPU architecture.

This methodology provides a critical blueprint for overcoming VRAM limitations by effectively mixing different NVIDIA GPU generations. For Senior Engineers, it’s a valuable guide for designing and implementing cost-effective local LLM inference solutions, allowing the utilization of existing or mixed GPU assets to run larger models that would otherwise exceed single-card VRAM capacity. This approach is highly relevant for internal AI development, rapid prototyping, or deploying privacy-sensitive LLM workloads where cloud solutions are not viable.

### Orthodox C++ (2016)
**Link:** https://bkaradzic.github.io/posts/orthodoxc++/

Shifting gears from hardware optimization to fundamental software principles, "Orthodox C++" presents a compelling philosophy for C++ development. It advocates for a minimal subset of the language, intentionally designed to enhance C without incorporating what it deems "unnecessary" or overly complex features of Modern C++. This approach prioritizes a simpler, C-like programming style that emphasizes readability, reduces complexity, and ensures broad compatibility across diverse compilers and projects.

The significance of Orthodox C++ for engineering lies in its promotion of highly maintainable, predictable, and portable codebases. By consciously avoiding features that introduce hidden runtime costs, excessive complexity, or build system challenges, it aims to drastically reduce technical debt and lower the barrier for collaboration and integration, especially critical in resource-constrained or cross-platform environments. Practical application involves primarily writing C-like code, eschewing C++ features like exceptions, RTTI, and `iostream`. Instead, developers are encouraged to prefer C standard library headers (e.g., `stdio.h`), use `printf`-style functions for I/O, and exercise caution with memory-allocating STL containers unless fine-grained memory control isn't a concern. The philosophy also advises prudence with newer C++ standard features and modules due to their potential to introduce complexity or portability issues.

### Every Frame Perfect
**Link:** https://tonsky.me/blog/every-frame-perfect/

Beyond computational efficiency and code robustness, user experience stands as a paramount factor in application success. The "every frame perfect" principle, as articulated in this article, demands that every momentary screenshot of an application, including during transitions and dynamic states, must appear coherent, complete, and free of visual imperfections. This philosophy necessitates meticulous attention to detail in UI rendering, aiming to eliminate visual glitches such as partial loads, inconsistent element states, desynchronized animations, and any "jank." It underscores that all dynamic UI states must be as polished as static ones.

For engineers, this concept highlights that UI polish is a direct reflection of application quality and a critical element in building user trust. It mandates a robust front-end architecture capable of precise state management, synchronized rendering across components, and careful handling of asynchronous data loading and animations. This proactive approach helps prevent "technology outsmarting the programmer" by systematically addressing potential visual glitches arising from complex interactions. In practical projects, engineers should adopt stringent UI rendering standards, focusing on atomic and synchronized updates for all UI elements. This includes implementing strategies like placeholder content or skeleton loaders to prevent incomplete states, meticulously designing and testing animations for smoothness and meaningfulness, and performing thorough QA across *intermediate* UI states, not just the final ones, to catch any visual inconsistencies.

### Derivative-Free Neural Network Optimization: MNIST Case **Link:** https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1u4fc16/derivative-free_neural_network_optimization_mnist/

Finally, pushing the boundaries of artificial intelligence, this paper delves into derivative-free optimization techniques for training neural networks. Unlike traditional backpropagation, which relies on gradient calculations, methods such as Evolution Strategies or Bayesian Optimization directly search the parameter space for optimal weights without computing gradients. The work successfully demonstrates this approach's effectiveness on the MNIST dataset, achieving competitive classification performance, proving a robust alternative for specific scenarios.

This approach holds significant technical importance for situations where gradient calculation is intractable, computationally expensive, or yields noisy results. Such scenarios include hardware-constrained environments, black-box optimization problems, or networks incorporating non-differentiable components. It offers a robust alternative to backpropagation, potentially improving the exploration of complex loss landscapes and enabling broader application contexts for neural networks. Engineers can leverage derivative-free optimization for training neural networks on specialized hardware (e.g., FPGAs, neuromorphic chips) where gradient computation is costly or impossible. It is also highly applicable in reinforcement learning to optimize policies with non-differentiable reward functions, for robust optimization in adversarial settings, or for black-box neural architecture search where objective functions are opaque.
